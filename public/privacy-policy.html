GRIND.

Privacy Policy

Last updated 22 September 2026

GRIND is a private, invite-only training journal run by an individual, not a company. It exists to show you your own training data. It is not an advertising product and there is nothing to sell.

The short version: GRIND stores what you log and, if you connect it, what your Fitbit records. That data is shown back to you and to nobody else. There are no analytics, no trackers, no advertising, and your data is never sold or shared for marketing.

What GRIND stores

  • Account — your email address and a display name. Passwords are handled by Supabase Auth and are never visible to the app.
  • Training logs — sessions you record: workout type, focus areas, duration, notes, meals, supplement amounts, dates, and any progress photos you attach.
  • Tracked lifts — exercises, sets, reps and weights you enter.
  • Preferences — units, week start, goals, theme, and which notifications you have switched on.
  • Fitbit / Google Health data, only if you connect it — workouts, sleep, heart rate, heart rate variability, steps, activity and body measurements.
  • Notification registration, only if you enable it — the push endpoint your browser issues, its encryption keys, your time zone, and your browser's user-agent string.

Why

Solely to operate the app for you: to display your history and progress, to send the notifications you asked for, and to keep you signed in. GRIND does not profile you, does not run analytics, and does not use your data to train anything.

Google Health / Fitbit data

Connecting Fitbit is optional and the access is read-only. GRIND requests only the scopes needed for the features you see: activity and fitness, sleep, and body measurements.

GRIND's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. That data is used only to provide the features you can see in the app. It is never sold, never used for advertising, and never shared with third parties except the infrastructure providers listed below.

Access tokens are held server-side and never sent to your browser. You can revoke access at any time from GRIND's settings, or from your Google Account permissions page.

Where it is stored, and who else touches it

GRIND is small and uses few services. Each one only ever sees what it needs to do its job:

  • Supabase — database, authentication and photo storage. This is where your data lives.
  • Vercel — serves the website itself.
  • Google — sign-in to Fitbit and the Google Health API, only if you connect it.
  • Your browser's push service (Apple, Google or Mozilla, depending on your device) — delivers notifications if you enable them. Notification text is encrypted so the push service cannot read it.
  • Google Fonts — serves the typefaces used by the site.

Access is enforced per row in the database, so one account cannot read another's data.

Notifications

Notifications are off until you switch them on, per type. Turning them off in settings, or removing the app from your home screen, ends them. Their content is limited to short summaries of your own activity.

Getting your data out, and deleting it

Settings → Data exports everything as JSON, and your sessions as CSV, at any time. To have your account and all associated data deleted, email the address below and it will be removed. Disconnecting Fitbit deletes the stored tokens immediately.

Retention

Your logs are kept until you delete them or ask for the account to be removed. Records of which notifications were already sent are pruned after 60 days.

Children

GRIND is not intended for anyone under 16 and accounts are issued by invitation only.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Material changes affecting how your data is handled will be sent to the email on your account.
